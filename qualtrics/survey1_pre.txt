<h3>Consent to participate in research</h3>
<p><i>REPLACE THIS ENTIRE BLOCK WITH YOUR IRB-APPROVED CONSENT LANGUAGE. The notes below are only a checklist of what typically has to appear, and of one wording constraint specific to this study.</i></p>
<ul>
<li>Study title and purpose &mdash; describe it as a study of <b>how people perceive sequences of everyday activities</b>. See the warning below.</li>
<li>Principal investigator, faculty advisor, department, contact details</li>
<li>IRB protocol number and IRB contact information</li>
<li>What participants will do: watch a series of pictures and make simple judgements, then answer some questions. About 25 minutes.</li>
<li>Compensation: course credit through SONA</li>
<li>Risks (minimal) and benefits</li>
<li>Confidentiality: responses are identified only by a random code; data are stored on the Open Science Framework in a private project</li>
<li>Voluntary participation, right to withdraw at any time without penalty</li>
<li>Statement that participants will receive a fuller explanation of the study's purpose at the end (incomplete disclosure + debriefing)</li>
</ul>
<p><b>WORDING CONSTRAINT &mdash; do not skip this.</b> The consent form must <b>not</b> use the words <i>memory</i>, <i>remember</i>, or <i>recall</i>. Encoding in this study is incidental and the memory test is a surprise; telling participants beforehand that memory is involved changes what is being measured and invalidates the design. Every other participant-facing screen has been kept neutral for the same reason. Use "how people perceive sequences of everyday activities" or equivalent, and declare the incomplete disclosure and the debriefing in your IRB protocol.</p>
